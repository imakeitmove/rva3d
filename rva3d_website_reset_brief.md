# RVA3D website: art-direction reset

**Version:** 0.1 — proposed direction for review  
**Date:** September 7, 2026  
**Purpose:** Turn the existing site into a coherent, work-first sales presentation without rebuilding the brand or starting another open-ended interaction project.

## 1. Status and evidence

This brief is a recommendation, not an approved redesign or a completed visual audit. It draws on Deven’s September 7 feedback, the two supplied Logo 010 treatments, the current RVA3D.com Notion checkpoint, the career roadmap, and the reference pages below. The exact latest local build has not been visually tested across devices for this brief. Reference-site conversion performance is unknown; admiration is not evidence of commercial effectiveness.

### Keep, per Deven

- The current reel and the effective “If a picture…” tagline pairing.
- “The proof is in the pixels.” and “Add dimension to your work.”
- The approved brand palette, current text-only site-header logo, and Logo 010 lettering.
- The successful direction of the capabilities page: relevant finished examples and specific capability descriptions.
- The human, slightly playful voice. Do not substitute inflated agency language or an artificial large-team persona.

Logo 010 uses purchased Not Just Groovy lettering for “3D,” with expanded outlines to relate to RVA. Do not reopen the lettering, launch another font search, or rebuild the approved header mark. Rendered marks and small navigation marks can serve different purposes.

### Proposed changes, requiring approval

- Bring selected finished projects directly after the reel/hero.
- Remove the ribbon from the main sales journey and remove mandatory Concept → WIP → Final reveals.
- Default project presentations to final media. Preserve optional process material where it helps a buyer understand a decision.
- Use Paper for reading/browsing areas and Void for selected viewing areas; do not replace one undifferentiated black page with arbitrary alternating color bands.
- Replace empty introductory states, redundant explanations, and specialist-first navigation with immediately useful content.
- Build a very small set of polished templates rather than a larger feature inventory.

## 2. Commercial objective

A visitor should quickly understand what RVA3D makes, identify a relevant example, understand what working together involves, and know how to make contact. The site must support both a leisurely viewer and a producer/brand-side buyer checking fit quickly.

Lead with the proven motion/3D/design work. Show interactive and technical range where a real example supports it. Present a senior-led studio, not a fictional large agency and not merely a freelancer’s software list.

Judge features by whether they improve viewing, orientation, project relevance, trust, or contact. A feature that needs a spoken explanation before it makes sense is a poor default for this sales site.

## 4. Proposed art direction

**A work-first editorial portfolio with cinematic media and a little physicality.**

The interface should provide a clear frame; Deven’s work should provide most of the spectacle and color. This is not a request for a beige luxury template, a SaaS card grid, or an all-black WebGL playground.

### Surfaces and color

Use the approved tokens already in the repository. Do not guess hex values from rendered artwork.

- Paper: the main browsing/reading surface, unless the approved composition demonstrates a better alternative.
- Void: reel and intentional screening areas, with enough separation to register as a change of viewing mode.
- Signal Green: a recognizable action/selection accent and a limited number of larger brand moments. Do not color arbitrary words simply to distribute green throughout a page.
- Purple: retain its established secondary role where useful, especially capability selection. It need not appear in every section.
- Other approved colors: available for project art and specific purposeful uses, not an interface checklist.

Use a single consistent frame, gutter system, caption alignment, and link language across differently colored projects. Dark content is not inherently a problem; lack of hierarchy and contrast between sections is.

### Typography and layout

Retain the established usable text family until a real composition reveals a specific problem. Reserve distinctive logo lettering for the mark and a very limited brand role. Use a small hierarchy: display, section heading, body, metadata.

Use a constrained desktop content width with meaningful outer gutters, a repeatable two-column project grid, and a single-column phone layout. Allow one featured project to be substantially larger than the others. Avoid equal emphasis on every item, decorative cards around every paragraph, and accidental line breaks in key copy.

Exact dimensions, font sizes, and spacing become fixed after the approved desktop/mobile composition; do not treat adjectives such as “premium” as implementation specifications.

### Media

Choose covers deliberately rather than allowing an arbitrary video frame to become a thumbnail. Prefer a consistent landscape cover ratio for comparable project cards; store a manual focal point when necessary. Preserve original framing in the full viewer. Design all layouts with actual available imagery, not glamorous placeholders that the real work cannot match.

Use rendered brand assets as authored imagery, not as a reason to extrude every interface element. Reuse existing good assets before commissioning new renders.

## 5. Proposed homepage

### Header and hero

Keep the accepted header mark. Navigation: Work, Capabilities, About, Contact. Keep direct navigation usable independently of animation.

Retain the reel and the approved “If a picture…” setup/payoff relationship. Add only the plain-language identification needed to understand the business. Offer an obvious deliberate way to play the reel with sound. Do not gate the page behind a loader, click-to-enter screen, completed playback, or scroll performance.

### Selected projects — immediately next

“The proof is in the pixels.” becomes the heading over actual finished work, not the introduction to an interaction tutorial.

Start with three editorially selected projects: one prominent feature and two supporting projects. Each must show a finished cover/preview, clear client/project identification, one concrete line of context where useful, and a direct project link. Do not require a hover, drag, or category choice to discover what an item is.

The current Notion evidence packets identify Cable Snake as the leading first-case candidate, with AMSOIL and the Capri Sun suite also staged. These are **candidates, not cleared public selections**. Confirm media quality, exact credits, and publication permission. Keep rights-pending material in the existing private review workflow. Do not delay all layout work while perfecting every candidate asset.

### Capabilities

Preserve the current page’s successful examples and clear capability breakdown. On the homepage, show a short useful selection of those examples, not a second sprawling interactive gallery. The first state must contain an actual capability and proof. Remove the “here’s what we do” cover state.

Match capabilities to work that supports them. Do not invent new services because they fit a symmetrical layout. “More ways to bring ideas to life.” remains a useful existing heading.

### Working together / about

Combine a short, real introduction to Deven with practical collaboration information. Explain what the client brings, what RVA3D can own, where approvals happen, and what is delivered. Keep claims consistent with actual engagement terms and capacity.

Do not add an abstract “why us” manifesto before showing more proof.

### Contact

“Add dimension to your work.” can be the closing invitation. Make email and the existing inquiry path obvious. Describe what is useful to send without turning the first contact into a qualification obstacle course. Preserve clear error, sending, and confirmation states in the form.

## 6. Work and project-page templates

### Work index

Use predictable card alignment, consistent labeling, deliberate media selection, and a clear editorial order. No random sequencing in the primary sales gallery. No filter system until the number and diversity of projects justify it. Do not make users translate production categories into reasons to hire RVA3D.

Short experiments can live in a secondary collection. They need not disappear, nor does every clip need a full written case study.

### A project story

Recommended starting structure:

1. Final film or strongest finished visual, with a useful title.
2. A short brief: what needed to be shown and who it was for.
3. A small factual credit block: RVA3D’s role, collaborators/agency as applicable, deliverables.
4. Two or three well-chosen visual decisions explained in plain language, each adjacent to evidence.
5. Actual outcome, client quote, or deliverable summary when available. Omit unsupported outcome claims rather than filling an obligatory slot.
6. Optional process/BTS material, then another relevant project and contact.

Aim initially for roughly 150–300 words of main narrative, not a hard limit on a story that genuinely needs more. The primary reading order must work without opening optional material. Do not force every case into identical section lengths or invent numbers, testimonials, project ownership, deadlines, or approvals.

Use “for [brand], with [agency]” or the correct equivalent when necessary; studio positioning must not inflate authorship of previous collaborative work.

## 7. Copy direction

Keep the memorable headlines. Replace the language between them with the words Deven would use to explain the project to an intelligent client.

Before drafting a case, get a quick spoken or rough-text answer to: What did they need? What was your part? What made the work challenging? What did you decide? What was delivered? Edit that material; do not synthesize an impressive-sounding story from the project title.

Draft examples to establish tone, not approved copy:

- “Have a brief? Great. Have a rough idea? That works too.”
- “We settle the look before spending time on the final animation.”
- “You’ll see the work as it develops, with clear points to review and approve.”

Use the last two only when they accurately describe the offered workflow. Allow a dry aside where it comes naturally, but do not make every button or paragraph audition as a joke. Avoid generic promises about seamless journeys, elevated experiences, innovation, or measurable impact without evidence.

## 8. Motion and functionality

Make the static composition excellent first. Then add a small, repeatable motion vocabulary.

For version one, focus on purposeful media previews, a reliable film/image viewer, and clear hover/focus/press feedback. A later optional product interaction can demonstrate a service; it must not be required to understand the offer or contact the studio.

Use final posters immediately; progressively enhance with video. Keep labels visible. Stop unnecessary offscreen playback. Do not eagerly load every film or 3D asset. Preserve native vertical scrolling and predictable browser Back behavior. The interface must remain understandable when playback, animation, or JavaScript enhancement is unavailable.

Automatically moving content that runs for more than five seconds alongside other content needs a pause/stop/hide mechanism unless an applicable essential exception applies. Reduced-motion preference handling does not replace that requirement. See W3C below.

Test keyboard operation, focus visibility and return, touch targets, video controls, reduced motion, legibility, and actual phone use. Design an ordinary poster/inline fallback, not an empty black rectangle, for failed or blocked playback.

Performance goals should use measured page behavior. Google’s current good Core Web Vitals thresholds are LCP ≤2.5 seconds, INP ≤200 milliseconds, and CLS ≤0.1 at the 75th percentile, considered separately for mobile and desktop. Prelaunch lab results are diagnostic, not a substitute for field data.

References:
- https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pause-stop-hide.html
- https://web.dev/articles/vitals

## 9. Preserve existing engineering investment

The September 7 Notion checkpoint describes a reusable project-media registry and full-bleed viewer. Removing the ribbon presentation does **not** require discarding that infrastructure. Inventory and reuse what is reliable.

Preserve routes, media, typed content, metadata, contact behavior, permission controls, private previews, and unrelated working features. Keep experiments out of the default sales path rather than destructively deleting them. Do not silently expose rights-pending records or add them to public navigation/sitemaps.

Do not migrate frameworks or buy another platform merely to obtain a clearer layout. Reconsider the stack only if the inventory identifies a concrete maintenance or delivery problem.

## 10. Delivery sequence and approval gates

**Gate A — content and references.** Select the actual lead projects, choose the primary reference and its exact borrowed elements, record rights/credit gaps, and agree on the homepage order. Keep this to a focused selection exercise.

**Gate B — visual composition.** Produce one high-fidelity homepage composition at desktop and phone widths using actual assets, plus one representative project-page composition. This can be done in a design tool or an isolated static HTML prototype. A grayscale wireframe alone will not answer the art-direction question. Do not build the whole redesign before approval.

**Gate C — one working slice.** Implement the header/reel/featured-work sequence and one project page first. Test with real media and refine the shared rules before spreading them through the rest of the site.

**Gate D — expand and release.** Apply the approved pattern to Work and Capabilities, finish contact, verify rights and credits, then test and publish deliberately. Richer interaction is optional follow-on work, not a prerequisite for release.

A bounded outside design engagement is a valid alternative to doing Gate B alone: request homepage and project-template art direction, not another brand system or an agency-scale discovery project. Deven retains creative ownership and can use Codex for implementation.

## 11. Validation

Ask a few people resembling actual buyers to identify what RVA3D does, choose a relevant project, describe RVA3D’s role in it, and find the contact path without a tour. Observe hesitation and wrong assumptions rather than coaching them through the page. This is qualitative feedback, not a statistically conclusive conversion experiment.

Use design-savvy reviewers for composition, typography, pacing, and media quality; use prospective buyers for relevance, trust, and hiring clarity. Do not confuse the two questions.

Success is stronger qualified conversations supported by clear work, not more time trapped in the page or praise for a complicated interaction.
